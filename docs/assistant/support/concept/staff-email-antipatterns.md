# What should staff avoid saying in external technical-support emails?

## Short Answer

Avoid unsupported promises, invented URLs or contacts, unexplained jargon, false certainty, and claims that a report automatically changes a dataset. External emails should separate confirmed facts, assumptions, and next steps.

## Significance

Support email becomes part of the partner's working record. Precise language reduces rework and prevents an informal answer from being treated as an official commitment.

## What This Means

- Do not promise a release date, fix, access decision, or feature availability without evidence.
- Do not expose internal information or credentials.
- Do not collapse distinct products, datasets, or workflows into one claim.
- Provide a useful verification or follow-up path.

## What This Does Not Mean

Avoiding an overclaim does not require an unhelpful or evasive message. Staff can give a bounded answer while identifying what remains to verify.

## How To Use This

Review the message for evidence, scope, audience, and commitments before sending. Replace speculation with a specific question, source, or next step.

## Example

Instead of saying a correction will be live next week, staff describe the current review stage and say that release timing needs confirmation.

## Assistant Guidance

Flag unsupported claims and avoid repeating them in polished language. Cite current documentation and abstain from filling missing operational details.
