# How do I know whether a dataset is authoritative?

## Short Answer

Determine a dataset's authority from its publisher, governance, scope, review or release status, provenance, and intended use. A dataset can be official for one purpose or jurisdiction without being authoritative for every feature or decision.

## Significance

Authority affects how an agency may rely on data and what review is still required. Making the basis of authority visible helps users distinguish a released source from a reference layer, draft, model, or community observation.

## What This Means

- Identify the publisher or steward and the dataset's geographic and thematic scope.
- Check provenance, release status, version, review information, and stated intended use.
- Compare the source with the authority required for the decision.
- Document limitations and seek local confirmation when the decision is high stakes.

## What This Does Not Mean

Public availability does not automatically make a dataset authoritative. A current release is not necessarily complete, and an authoritative source for one attribute may not be authoritative for another.

## How To Use This

Record the dataset name, publisher, version or date, scope, and authority statement in project materials. Treat imported or reference data as inputs until the responsible steward confirms their status.

## Example

A planner compares a released pedestrian dataset with a local curb-ramp inventory, records each source's publisher and date, and asks the jurisdiction which source governs the capital decision.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not label a dataset authoritative without source or governance evidence. Ask what jurisdiction, attribute, and decision are involved, cite the dataset metadata, and abstain when authority cannot be verified.
