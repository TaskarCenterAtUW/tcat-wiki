# How should staff explain uncertainty without sounding evasive?

## Short Answer

Explain what is known, what is uncertain, why the uncertainty matters, and what would verify it. Pair the limitation with a useful next step instead of filling the gap with speculation.

## Significance

Clear uncertainty protects trust and helps partners decide whether to proceed, verify locally, or ask the responsible team.

## What This Means

- Lead with the best supported answer.
- Name the missing version, dataset, location, or source.
- State the practical consequence and provide a verification path.

## What This Does Not Mean

Uncertainty is not a refusal to help, and confidence in one part of an answer does not make every related claim certain.

## How To Use This

Use plain language such as "the current documentation confirms" and "this needs verification." Avoid vague hedging without explaining what is missing.

## Example

A staff member explains that a dataset's release date is known but its local currency is not, then recommends checking the jurisdiction's current records.

## Assistant Guidance

Be specific about uncertainty and cite the available evidence. Abstain from unsupported claims while still providing a bounded next step.
