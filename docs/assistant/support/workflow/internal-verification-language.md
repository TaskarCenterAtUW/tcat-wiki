# What language should staff use when a question requires internal verification?

## Short Answer

Say that the detail needs internal verification, identify what is being checked, and provide a bounded next step or follow-up path. Do not imply that an unverified answer is confirmed.

## Significance

Specific verification language is more useful than a vague promise to check and helps preserve trust when product or organizational details are changing.

## What This Means

- State the known portion of the answer.
- Name the missing fact and the responsible verification context.
- Avoid promising a response time unless one is documented.

## What This Does Not Mean

Verification language is not evidence that the answer will be confirmed or that the internal team has accepted responsibility for a particular outcome.

## How To Use This

Use a sentence such as: "I can confirm [known fact]. The current [version, ownership, or workflow] needs internal verification, so I do not want to guess."

## Example

A partner asks whether a particular portal permission is currently available. Staff explain the documented access model and say the live permission behavior needs internal verification.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not invent internal contacts, decisions, or timelines. Cite the known documentation, label the unresolved point, and route the question through the current support process.
