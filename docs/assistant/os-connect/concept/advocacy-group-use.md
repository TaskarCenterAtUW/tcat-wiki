# How can advocacy groups use OS-CONNECT?

## Short Answer

Advocacy groups can use OS-CONNECT to understand mapped pedestrian-network conditions, identify possible gaps, compare areas or destinations, support public education, and bring specific questions to agencies or data stewards. The data are evidence for discussion and analysis, not a final statement about community need.

## Significance

A common dataset can help groups make concrete, location-based observations and participate in planning conversations without requiring every group to build its own data system.

## What This Means

Groups can review coverage and connectivity, compare modeled access around important destinations, document locations for field verification, report data issues, and combine the dataset with community knowledge or other appropriate information.

## What This Does Not Mean

OS-CONNECT does not replace lived experience, establish legal compliance, guarantee that an agency will act, or prove that a mapped gap affects every person in the same way.

## How To Use This

State the question and geography, record the dataset version, distinguish mapped evidence from community reports, validate important claims locally, and use the current documented channel for corrections or agency follow-up.

## Example

An advocacy group uses a map to identify several possible crossing gaps near a community destination, checks the locations with residents, and presents the documented observations to the responsible agency for review.

## Assistant Guidance

Explain what the dataset can and cannot show. Do not present a model or one group's report as the voice of an entire community, and abstain when the source, version, or local context is unknown.
