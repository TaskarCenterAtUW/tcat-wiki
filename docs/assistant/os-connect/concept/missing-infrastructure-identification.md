# How can communities identify missing infrastructure?

## Short Answer

Communities can identify missing infrastructure by comparing maps and data with current observations, local knowledge, imagery, important destinations, and lived travel experience. A suspected gap should be documented for review rather than inferred from map absence alone.

## Significance

Community observation can reveal missing sidewalks, crossings, ramps, connections, or facilities that automated or historical data do not show.

## What This Means

Record the location, feature type, date, dataset release, observed condition, evidence, and effect on travel. Use a documented reporting or engagement pathway and include different community perspectives where possible.

## What This Does Not Mean

An absent feature does not prove that it is absent in the real world, inaccessible, or assigned to a particular agency. A report does not automatically update a release.

## How To Use This

Use accessible and privacy-conscious methods, validate important findings locally, distinguish temporary conditions from data gaps, and retain the source and follow-up status.

## Example

A group identifies a missing sidewalk connection near a transit stop, checks the release and current conditions, and submits a location-specific correction request with supporting evidence.

## Assistant Guidance

Explain the difference between map absence, reported absence, and verified absence. Cite the source and review process, and abstain when the location or evidence is not specific.
