# What role could crowdsourcing play?

## Short Answer

Crowdsourcing can expand the number of people who contribute observations, mapping, corrections, and local knowledge about pedestrian conditions. Contributions should be documented and reviewed before they are treated as accepted data.

## Significance

Distributed contributions can reveal conditions that centralized sources miss and help prioritize locations for review. They also vary in detail, consistency, and reliability.

## What This Means

Define the task, location, instructions, privacy expectations, schema, evidence requirements, and review process. Preserve contributor context and distinguish submitted observations from verified or released data.

## What This Does Not Mean

Crowdsourced data are not automatically complete, accurate, representative, authoritative, or suitable for every decision. Participation does not guarantee that a report will change a dataset.

## How To Use This

Use clear prompts and accessible tools, sample contributions for quality, provide correction and feedback paths, and match review effort to the consequence of the intended use.

## Example

A mapping project gathers reports of missing curb ramps, then reviewers compare the submissions with imagery and local records before using accepted findings in a planning analysis.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not equate the number of contributions with data quality or community consensus. Cite the collection and review process, protect privacy, and abstain when representativeness or validation is unknown.
