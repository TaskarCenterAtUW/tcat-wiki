# How does this improve accessibility?

## Short Answer

OS-CONNECT can improve accessibility indirectly by helping people and organizations see, analyze, validate, and prioritize information about pedestrian infrastructure. The data do not improve a physical condition until an appropriate organization uses verified findings to make a change.

## Significance

A shared dataset can reduce information gaps, support better questions, and connect community observations with planning or maintenance discussions.

## What This Means

Users may identify missing connections, compare modeled access, locate features for field review, report errors, and inform investment or stewardship decisions. The practical benefit depends on data quality, local action, and how results are communicated.

## What This Does Not Mean

Publishing a map does not repair infrastructure, guarantee an accessible route, or ensure that every barrier has been captured. OS-CONNECT is not a substitute for design, maintenance, field inspection, or legal review.

## How To Use This

Use the data to identify a bounded question and a next action. Record the source and version, validate important findings, involve affected users, and track whether a proposed change is actually completed.

## Example

A jurisdiction uses a connectivity review to identify a possible crossing gap, confirms the condition locally, and uses the verified finding to discuss maintenance or capital improvements.

## Assistant Guidance

Separate what the dataset shows from what an agency or community may do in response. Avoid claiming a direct accessibility improvement unless a completed physical or operational change has been verified.
