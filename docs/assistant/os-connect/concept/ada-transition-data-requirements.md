# What additional data is needed for ADA transition plans?

## Short Answer

OS-CONNECT may be one input to an ADA transition-planning process. Additional data commonly include the public entity's facilities and right-of-way responsibilities, inventories of barriers and accessible features, field evidence, ownership, priorities, costs, schedules, responsible parties, and records of public input. The required evidence depends on the jurisdiction and plan scope.

## Significance

A transition plan must connect observed barriers to responsibilities and actions. A pedestrian-network dataset alone generally cannot establish the legal, programmatic, facility, or implementation information needed for that work.

## What This Means

Combine the relevant OS-CONNECT release with authoritative local records, facility and right-of-way inventories, curb-ramp and sidewalk observations, ownership information, engineering or inspection evidence, prioritization criteria, and documented commitments. Identify data gaps and retain dates and sources.

## What This Does Not Mean

There is no universal checklist in this article, and the presence of these data does not make a plan legally sufficient. OS-CONNECT does not certify conditions, determine obligations, or replace legal and professional review.

## How To Use This

Define the plan's scope and responsible entity first. Use OS-CONNECT to screen or organize information, then have qualified staff determine which additional records, field work, public engagement, and documentation the plan requires.

## Example

An agency uses OS-CONNECT to flag possible gaps near public facilities, then joins those findings with its facility inventory, field observations, ownership records, cost estimates, and implementation schedule before formal review.

## Assistant Guidance

Ask for the jurisdiction, plan scope, dataset version, and decision being made. Cite current legal and agency guidance, describe OS-CONNECT as an input, and abstain from declaring a plan complete or compliant.
