# How are curb ramps identified?

## Short Answer

Curb ramps may be identified from mapped features, imagery, local inventories, field observations, or automated processing. The source and method determine what can be concluded, and inferred or detected ramps should be distinguished from verified features.

## Significance

Reliable identification supports crossing analysis and inventory review, but curb-ramp presence alone does not describe the ramp's condition or usability.

## What This Means

Check the source, collection date, geometry, evidence, attributes, and validation status. Review the ramp's relationship to the crossing and pedestrian approaches, not just the presence of a point or label.

## What This Does Not Mean

A source inventory identifies a curb ramp at an intersection, while imagery and field review confirm that it connects to the intended crossing and record any remaining unknown attributes.

## How To Use This

Identification does not establish that a ramp meets a design standard, is clear today, or serves every mobility need. A missing record does not prove that no ramp exists.

## Example

Use the release-specific definitions, preserve source and confidence information, verify important locations locally, and report omissions or false identifications through the current channel.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not claim that a particular method finds all ramps. State whether the feature is observed, inferred, or reported, cite the source, and abstain when evidence is insufficient.
