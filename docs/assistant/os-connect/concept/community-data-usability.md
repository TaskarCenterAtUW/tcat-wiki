# What makes community-mapped sidewalk data usable for agency workflows?

## Short Answer

Community-mapped sidewalk data can be considered for an agency workflow when its scope, provenance, mapping conventions, attributes, version, licensing, and review status are documented. The intended use and the agency's own requirements should determine the level of validation required; TCAT can provide the data and examples of possible uses but does not define an authoritative workflow for every agency.

## Significance

Agencies can benefit from local knowledge and volunteer capacity, but usable data must remain interpretable and traceable after handoff.

## What This Means

- Document who mapped the area, when, with which conventions and sources.
- Check geometry, connectivity, attributes, coverage, and licensing.
- Let the agency define review, acceptance, maintenance, and release responsibilities for its intended use.

## What This Does Not Mean

Usable does not mean complete, authoritative, error-free, or suitable for every decision. Community mapping does not remove the agency's responsibility to review data for its intended use.

## How To Use This

Request the source package and documentation, test a representative area, and label unverified or reference layers clearly. Apply the agency's own workflow and acceptance criteria rather than treating this article as an authoritative agency procedure.

## Example

A city receives volunteer sidewalk data with mapping dates and source notes, checks connectivity and attributes, and uses the reviewed subset in a planning layer.

## Assistant Guidance

Ask what agency decision or product will use the data. Cite the source and review status, explain that TCAT provides data and use examples rather than agency policy, and abstain from calling it authoritative when provenance or validation is missing.
