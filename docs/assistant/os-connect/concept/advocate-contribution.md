# How should accessibility advocates contribute?

## Short Answer

Accessibility advocates should contribute specific observations, priorities, and lived experience while distinguishing those contributions from what a dataset or model establishes. Their input can help identify locations for verification and improve the questions agencies ask.

## Significance

Advocates often notice barriers that are absent from structured data. Clear, location-specific contributions help data stewards and agencies investigate those gaps without treating a single report as a complete survey.

## What This Means

Describe the location, date, travel context, observed barrier, and available evidence. Participate in documented mapping, feedback, or planning channels, and say whether the information is an observation, a correction request, or a broader priority.

## What This Does Not Mean

An advocate's report does not automatically change a dataset, establish a legal violation, or represent the experience of every person in a community. Advocacy input does not replace field or professional review.

## How To Use This

Share only the information needed for review, ask how the report will be handled, retain the dataset version when discussing a map, and follow up through the verified support or engagement channel.

## Example

An advocate documents a crossing that is modeled as connected but has a barrier in the field. The report identifies the observation and location, and the responsible team determines whether a data correction or local action is appropriate.

## Assistant Guidance

Respect the advocate's stated experience, avoid generalizing it, and explain what remains to be verified. Do not promise acceptance or action when the workflow is undocumented.
