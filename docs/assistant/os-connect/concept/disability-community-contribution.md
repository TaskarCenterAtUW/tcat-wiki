# How can disability communities contribute?

## Short Answer

Disability communities can contribute lived experience, observations, priorities, accessibility requirements, review questions, and correction reports through documented participation channels.

## Significance

People who encounter barriers can identify conditions that structured data, imagery, or automated methods may miss. Their contributions improve the questions and locations selected for review.

## What This Means

Provide accessible ways to participate, describe the location and travel context, distinguish an observation from a correction request, protect privacy, and explain how the contribution will be reviewed.

## What This Does Not Mean

A contribution does not represent every disabled person, guarantee a dataset change, or replace field, engineering, legal, or agency review.

## How To Use This

Ask communities what information and participation format work for them. Preserve context, avoid unnecessary sensitive details, and communicate verified next steps without promising outcomes.

## Example

A person reports that a connected route is difficult to use because of a barrier not represented in the data. The report is retained as lived-experience evidence while the location is investigated separately.

## Assistant Guidance

Respect the contributor's experience without generalizing it. Cite the engagement or reporting process, distinguish experience from modeled data, and abstain when the current pathway is unknown.
