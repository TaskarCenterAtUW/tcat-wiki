# What governance models are possible?

## Short Answer

Possible governance models for OS-CONNECT-like data include centralized stewardship, distributed jurisdiction ownership with shared coordination, consortium or regional governance, community-supported review, and hybrid models. The appropriate model depends on authority, funding, data scope, and maintenance needs.

## Significance

Governance determines who can contribute, review, correct, publish, maintain, and explain data. A technically interoperable dataset still needs clear decisions and accountability.

## What This Means

Define roles for data ownership, stewardship, local validation, schema maintenance, issue resolution, release management, privacy, licensing, and communication. Document escalation and disagreement processes.

## What This Does Not Mean

No governance model automatically makes data complete, current, authoritative, or sustainable. A statewide publisher does not necessarily own or control every local source.

## How To Use This

Compare models against the intended use and legal or institutional context, pilot responsibilities, fund recurring work, and make handoffs and unresolved conflicts visible.

## Example

A shared program coordinates schema and releases while jurisdictions validate local conditions and communities report observations. Each role is documented rather than assumed.

## Assistant Guidance

Present models as options unless a current governance decision is documented. Do not assign ownership or promise sustainability without evidence.
