# What role could advocacy groups play?

## Short Answer

Advocacy groups can contribute lived experience, identify barriers and priorities, review interpretations, support community mapping, and help agencies understand how pedestrian conditions affect different people. Their role should be defined with the group rather than assumed from the data.

## Significance

Advocacy participation can improve the relevance and accountability of accessibility-data work, especially where mapped attributes do not capture practical experience.

## What This Means

Groups may help set mapping priorities, share observations, review proposed metrics, identify high-consequence locations, communicate findings, and participate in correction or planning discussions through documented channels.

## What This Does Not Mean

Participation does not make a group responsible for agency decisions, guarantee that every concern is accepted, or replace field, engineering, legal, or data review.

## How To Use This

Ask what role the group wants, provide accessible materials, explain uncertainty and decision boundaries, compensate or credit contributions appropriately where applicable, and report how feedback was considered.

## Example

An advocacy group identifies a crossing that appears connected in the data but is difficult to use in practice. The group shares the observation, the agency verifies the location, and the result informs data correction or further planning review.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not speak for an advocacy group or infer community priorities from a map. Cite the source of feedback, distinguish reported experience from modeled data, and abstain when the participation pathway is not documented.
