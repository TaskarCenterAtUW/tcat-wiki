# How should agencies use this data operationally?

## Short Answer

Agencies should use OS-CONNECT as a documented input for planning, screening, network analysis, prioritization, public engagement, and data review, with the exact use governed by the agency's own requirements and the dataset's quality and scope.

## Significance

Operational use can connect pedestrian data to real decisions while making uncertainty and maintenance needs visible.

## What This Means

Define the decision, geography, release, authority, metrics, acceptable uncertainty, local validation, responsible owner, and follow-up. Combine OS-CONNECT with authoritative local, field, transit, safety, or facility data as needed.

## What This Does Not Mean

OS-CONNECT does not replace an agency's asset inventory, inspection, engineering, legal, emergency, or public-process responsibilities. A map or metric is not automatically operationally authoritative.

## How To Use This

Start with a bounded pilot, record assumptions and limitations, validate consequential findings, track corrections, and revisit results when data or conditions change.

## Example

A city uses OS-CONNECT to screen sidewalk gaps near schools, validates priority locations, and adds the reviewed findings to a planning workflow without treating the release as a complete asset inventory.

## Assistant Guidance

Ask what decision and agency authority are involved. Cite the release and local sources, avoid promises about outcomes, and abstain when the data's scope or quality is unknown.
