# How can communities use walksheds to advocate for change?

## Short Answer

Communities can use walksheds to advocate for change by showing modeled access, possible network gaps, and scenario differences around destinations that matter to them.

## Significance

A clear map can help turn a general concern into a location-specific question for agencies, designers, or data stewards.

## What This Means

Record the origin, destinations, profile, travel limit, network, release, and limitations. Combine the output with lived experience, local evidence, safety, cost, feasibility, and community priorities.

## What This Does Not Mean

A group compares reachability before and after a proposed crossing, validates the location, and presents the result as evidence for discussion rather than as proof of a required project.

## How To Use This

A walkshed does not prove inequity, guarantee agency action, or represent every resident. It is not a legal, safety, or accessibility certification.

## Example

Explain uncertainty publicly, invite affected people to interpret results, report data gaps, and distinguish advocacy priorities from verified physical conditions.

## Assistant Guidance

Name the settings and sources, avoid overstating modeled benefits, and abstain when the output or comparison is not reproducible.
