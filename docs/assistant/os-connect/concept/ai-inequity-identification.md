# Can AI help identify accessibility inequities?

## Short Answer

AI can help screen datasets for possible differences in pedestrian coverage, modeled access, or reported barriers, but it cannot determine accessibility inequity automatically. Any finding depends on the data, comparison, assumptions, and community context.

## Significance

Automated analysis may help identify places for deeper review when staff have more locations than they can inspect manually. It can also amplify uneven data coverage or encode assumptions that disadvantage less-observed communities.

## What This Means

Define the equity question, comparison groups, geography, dataset version, and measures first. Review outputs for missing data and disparate errors, involve affected communities, and validate important findings locally.

## What This Does Not Mean

An AI-generated pattern is not proof of inequity, discrimination, or community need. It does not speak for residents, replace participatory work, or determine an agency's legal or funding obligations.

## How To Use This

Use AI output as a screening aid and publish its sources, assumptions, limitations, and review process. Keep possible findings separate from confirmed observations and provide a way to correct the underlying data.

## Example

A screening model identifies lower mapped connectivity near several destinations in one area. Analysts check coverage and demographic assumptions, discuss the result with residents, and verify whether a physical or data gap exists.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not infer inequity from one score or automated pattern. Cite the data and method, state what was validated, and abstain when the comparison basis or community evidence is missing.
