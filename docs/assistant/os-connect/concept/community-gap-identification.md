# How can communities identify accessibility gaps?

## Short Answer

Communities can identify possible accessibility gaps by comparing mapped coverage and connectivity with lived experience, local knowledge, field observations, reported barriers, and important destinations. A gap should be documented as a question for review rather than assumed from one map.

## Significance

Community-led identification can surface missing infrastructure, missing attributes, disconnected features, or conditions that automated data do not show.

## What This Means

Choose a location and question, record the dataset version, compare the map with observations, document the barrier or missing information, and submit corrections or planning feedback through the current channel.

## What This Does Not Mean

An apparent data gap does not prove that infrastructure is absent, inaccessible, or the responsibility of a particular agency. Community reports do not automatically change a release.

## How To Use This

Use accessible mapping and feedback methods, include people with different experiences, protect privacy, and distinguish mapped evidence, reported experience, and verified field conditions.

## Example

A group notices that a route to a community center appears connected in the dataset but requires an unsafe crossing. Members document the location and experience, then request local verification.

## Assistant Guidance

State what the map shows and what the community observed. Cite the release, avoid claiming that one report represents everyone, and abstain when the location or evidence is not sufficiently specific.
