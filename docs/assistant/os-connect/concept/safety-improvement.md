# How does this improve safety?

## Short Answer

OS-CONNECT can support safety work by making pedestrian-network geometry, crossings, barriers, and possible gaps available for screening, analysis, and review. Data alone do not improve safety; verified findings must inform appropriate local action.

## Significance

Shared pedestrian information can help agencies and communities identify locations for design, maintenance, or field investigation.

## What This Means

Use the data alongside crash, traffic, design, lighting, transit, field, and community evidence. Record the release and assumptions and distinguish a network gap from a safety finding.

## What This Does Not Mean

OS-CONNECT does not measure all crash risk, certify a route as safe, or prove that a project will prevent harm. A map or metric is not an engineering determination.

## How To Use This

Use it as one input to a bounded safety question, validate current conditions locally, involve affected communities, and obtain appropriate professional review.

## Example

A planner identifies a crossing gap near a school, checks current design and traffic conditions, and sends the location for a separate safety and accessibility assessment.

## Assistant Guidance

Avoid causal or definitive safety claims. Cite the data and safety sources, state uncertainty, and abstain when current field evidence is unavailable.
