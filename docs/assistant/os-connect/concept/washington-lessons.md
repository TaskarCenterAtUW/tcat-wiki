# What lessons has Washington learned?

## Short Answer

Washington's OS-CONNECT work illustrates the value of shared pedestrian-network data, explicit quality review, and a process for tracking corrections through later releases. The lessons are operational and context-dependent rather than a universal prescription for every jurisdiction.

## Significance

Statewide or multi-jurisdictional network data requires coordination among publishers, reviewers, and users. Without maintenance and release practices, useful accessibility information can become difficult to trust or act on.

## What This Means

 - Treat data collection, QA/QC, correction tracking, and release as connected activities.
 - Make dataset scope, provenance, and known gaps visible to users.
 - Adapt lessons to local governance, resources, and data conditions.

## What This Does Not Mean

Washington's experience does not show that one workflow or release cadence works everywhere. It also does not make OS-CONNECT data a substitute for local validation or policy decisions.

## How To Use This

Use the lessons to frame planning and stewardship questions, then verify current OS-CONNECT documentation and local operating practices before adopting a workflow.

## Example

A jurisdiction uses a QA/QC finding to open a correction workflow, documents its review status, and checks the next release rather than assuming the map changed immediately.

## Assistant Guidance

Describe these as documented lessons and operational patterns, not guarantees. Cite applicable release or workflow evidence and identify jurisdictional gaps.
