# How could accessibility stewardship become sustainable?

## Short Answer

Accessibility stewardship could become sustainable through recurring funding, clear roles, shared standards, maintainable tools, local and community partnerships, issue and release workflows, documentation, and evaluation of actual use and workload.

## Significance

Sustainability requires support after initial collection so that data, software, governance, and relationships continue to function as conditions change.

## What This Means

Plan for maintenance costs, staff capacity, source updates, validation, privacy and licensing, accessible participation, technical upgrades, correction tracking, and succession when a project or grant ends.

## What This Does Not Mean

No funding model, partnership, or automation guarantees long-term sustainability, and a sustainable process does not guarantee complete or current data.

## How To Use This

Make recurring responsibilities explicit, pilot the workload, measure unresolved issues and release lag, and preserve documentation and ownership before scaling.

## Example

A program combines state coordination, jurisdictional validation, community reporting, technical maintenance, and recurring budget support instead of relying only on an initial grant.

## Assistant Guidance

Present sustainability ideas as options unless a current commitment exists. Do not promise funding or continuity, and cite verified governance information.
