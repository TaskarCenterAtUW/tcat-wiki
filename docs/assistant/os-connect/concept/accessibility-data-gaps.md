# What gaps still exist in accessibility data?

## Short Answer

Accessibility data can still have gaps in coverage, connectivity, feature attributes, currency, provenance, and validation. The specific gaps depend on the dataset, location, collection method, and release.

## Significance

Knowing where information is incomplete helps users interpret maps and analyses responsibly. It also helps jurisdictions and communities identify places where additional mapping, review, or field work may be useful.

## What This Means

Gaps may include unmapped sidewalks or crossings, disconnected geometry, missing curb-ramp or surface information, outdated imagery, and conditions that cannot be observed reliably from available sources. A completeness measure describes mapped data, not every real-world accessibility condition.

## What This Does Not Mean

An incomplete field or network does not prove that infrastructure is absent or inaccessible. A complete-looking map does not establish ADA compliance, current usability, or equal experience for every traveler.

## How To Use This

Check the dataset release, geographic scope, collection date, field definitions, and quality or confidence information. Record the gap, verify high-consequence locations locally, and report corrections through the current documented channel.

## Example

A map may show a connected sidewalk network while lacking curb-ramp details at several crossings. The missing detail identifies a data gap for review; it does not by itself establish that the crossings lack ramps.

## Assistant Guidance

Describe the specific missing coverage, field, or validation evidence. Cite the dataset and version, distinguish data limitations from physical conditions, and abstain from definitive accessibility or compliance claims when the evidence is incomplete.
