# How can advocacy organizations participate?

## Short Answer

Advocacy organizations can participate by sharing lived experience, identifying locations for review, contributing observations or corrections, helping set priorities, and reviewing how accessibility data are interpreted. The appropriate role depends on the organization's goals and the current documented engagement pathway.

## Significance

Participation can make data work more relevant to people who experience barriers that are not fully represented in a dataset. It can also improve accountability when agencies explain how feedback informed review or planning.

## What This Means

Organizations may contribute through community mapping, issue reports, workshops, public comment, field validation, or planning discussions. They should be told what question is being considered, what the data can show, and how their input will be used.

## What This Does Not Mean

Participation does not guarantee that every report is accepted, that an agency will fund a project, or that a community organization speaks for every disabled person or resident.

## How To Use This

Use accessible materials and documented channels, identify the location and observation, distinguish lived experience from modeled data, and request a response about how the input was reviewed. Preserve privacy and do not submit sensitive information unnecessarily.

## Example

An organization reports that a mapped connection is difficult to use despite appearing connected. The data steward records the observation, the responsible agency verifies the site, and both the report and the verification outcome are kept distinct.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not promise an outcome or infer a group's priorities. Cite the relevant participation or reporting guidance, explain the review boundary, and abstain when the current engagement path is not known.
