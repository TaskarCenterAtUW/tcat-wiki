# Can mapping volunteers contribute data that agencies can use?

## Short Answer

Mapping volunteers can contribute pedestrian data that agencies may use after the data are documented, reviewed, and validated for the intended workflow. Volunteer contribution can expand coverage, but it does not automatically make a feature authoritative or ready for publication.

## Significance

Community mapping can provide local observations and extend capacity beyond agency staff. A clear review boundary helps agencies benefit from those contributions without confusing participation with official acceptance.

## What This Means

- Volunteers should follow the project's mapping conventions and document the area and source information.
- Agencies can review volunteer data for completeness, consistency, provenance, and local relevance.
- Accepted data may support analysis or a later release through the applicable stewardship process.

## What This Does Not Mean

Volunteer data are not automatically authoritative, legally sufficient, or publication-ready. A contribution does not guarantee acceptance, correction, or synchronization with every related dataset.

## How To Use This

Define the intended agency use before mapping begins, provide consistent instructions, and establish who will review and maintain the result. Preserve contributor and source information when transferring data into an agency workflow.

## Example

A volunteer group maps sidewalk connections in a neighborhood. An agency reviews the edits against local knowledge and imagery before using the verified subset in a planning workflow.

## Assistant Guidance

Explain that volunteer contributions can be useful inputs but require review. Cite the project's mapping and validation guidance, and abstain from calling a contribution authoritative without evidence of agency acceptance or release.
