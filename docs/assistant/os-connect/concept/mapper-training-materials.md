# What training materials are available for new sidewalk mappers?

## Short Answer

New sidewalk mappers should use the current project instructions, OpenSidewalks or OSM documentation, editor guidance, and any available TCAT or community training sessions. The correct materials depend on the mapping tool, schema, area, and intended use.

## Significance

Consistent training improves feature interpretation, tagging, and review. It also gives volunteers a clear way to ask questions instead of encoding uncertain assumptions in the data.

## What This Means

- Start with the project's current scope, conventions, and examples.
- Practice in the intended editor with feedback from an experienced reviewer.
- Review geometry, crossings, attributes, and connectivity before contributing at scale.
- Keep training and source versions documented.

## What This Does Not Mean

Training is not a substitute for local verification or review. Completing a lesson does not make every edit accurate, authoritative, or ready for agency use.

## How To Use This

Ask the project owner which materials and editor are current, then begin with a small practice area and review the result before expanding.

## Example

A new volunteer reads the project instructions, practices mapping a short sidewalk segment and crossing, receives review feedback, and then continues with the larger task area.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not invent a current course, link, certification, or training requirement. Ask for the project and tool, cite available instructions, and abstain when current materials are not identified.
