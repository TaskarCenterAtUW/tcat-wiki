# What is the relationship between local ownership and statewide coordination?

## Short Answer

Local ownership and statewide coordination are complementary: local jurisdictions may hold current operational knowledge and responsibilities, while statewide coordination can provide shared schemas, release practices, interoperability, and cross-boundary consistency.

## Significance

Pedestrian conditions and data responsibilities cross jurisdictional boundaries. Coordination can reduce gaps without erasing local authority or source provenance.

## What This Means

Define which organization owns or maintains each source, who reviews local conditions, how changes are submitted, how conflicts are resolved, and how accepted changes enter a shared release.

## What This Does Not Mean

Statewide coordination does not make one organization authoritative for every local feature, and local ownership does not guarantee that data are synchronized or complete statewide.

## How To Use This

Use local records for current operational decisions, shared releases for documented regional analysis, and clear metadata and handoffs when moving between them.

## Example

A county coordinates schema and release work while cities validate local crossings and retain responsibility for their current operational records.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not assign ownership without evidence. Distinguish owner, steward, publisher, and contributor, cite governance guidance, and abstain when responsibilities are unclear.
