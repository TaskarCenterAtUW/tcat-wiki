# What are the biggest barriers to adoption?

## Short Answer

Common barriers to adopting connected pedestrian data include unclear ownership, inconsistent schemas, incomplete or outdated coverage, limited staff capacity, uncertainty about quality, integration costs, licensing constraints, and difficulty converting analysis into funded action. The barriers vary by agency, community, and use case.

## Significance

Recognizing adoption barriers helps teams plan support and avoid treating a data download as the same thing as sustained operational use.

## What This Means

An agency may need governance, documentation, training, technical integration, quality review, maintenance funding, accessibility expertise, and a process for community or jurisdiction corrections. Different use cases require different levels of assurance.

## What This Does Not Mean

No single barrier explains every adoption decision, and a tool or dataset cannot remove organizational, legal, financial, or physical constraints automatically.

## How To Use This

Start with a bounded decision and identify the minimum data, review, staff, and maintenance requirements. Pilot the workflow, document limitations, involve intended users, and define who owns follow-up before scaling.

## Example

An agency can view a pedestrian dataset but lacks a maintenance owner and a documented quality process. It addresses those governance gaps before using the data in a recurring planning workflow.

## Assistant Guidance

Ask what adoption means, for whom, and for which decision. Avoid claiming that OS-CONNECT solves an agency's workflow or resource barriers without evidence.
