# Who is responsible for long-term stewardship?

## Short Answer

Responsibility for long-term stewardship is role-dependent. Data publishers, technical stewards, jurisdictions, contributors, communities, and downstream users may each have different responsibilities for maintenance, validation, correction, release, or interpretation.

## Significance

Clear responsibility prevents a shared dataset from becoming an orphaned product after initial funding or collection ends.

## What This Means

Identify the source owner, publisher, steward, local authority, reviewers, and users. Document who handles reports, validates changes, manages releases, maintains tools, and communicates limitations.

## What This Does Not Mean

The organization hosting a viewer does not automatically own every source or physical condition. Community contributors and users do not automatically assume maintenance responsibility.

## How To Use This

Use release metadata and governance records to identify the responsible role, route corrections appropriately, and retain local operational data when it is needed for current decisions.

## Example

A jurisdiction maintains current local records while a shared steward manages release coordination. Both roles are recorded so users know where to send operational questions and dataset corrections.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not name an owner without evidence. Distinguish ownership, stewardship, publication, and contribution, cite governance documentation, and abstain when responsibility is not documented.
