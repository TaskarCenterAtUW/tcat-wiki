# What role did imagery play?

## Short Answer

Imagery can provide visual evidence for mapping pedestrian features, checking geometry, identifying possible barriers, and prioritizing review. Its usefulness depends on resolution, coverage, date, source, licensing, and the feature being examined.

## Significance

Imagery can extend collection and review across large areas, but it cannot reliably show every accessibility or current-condition detail.

## What This Means

Record the imagery source and date, inspect visibility and resolution, compare imagery with other evidence, and send uncertain or important locations for local or field validation.

## What This Does Not Mean

Imagery is not a field survey, guarantee of current conditions, or proof of accessibility, ownership, or compliance. Clear imagery does not mean that every feature was detected.

## How To Use This

Use imagery as one source, account for change and occlusion, preserve licensing and provenance, and label inferred or image-based results appropriately.

## Example

An image shows a possible curb ramp but a parked vehicle obscures its connection. The feature is recorded as a review candidate rather than accepted without further evidence.

## Assistant Guidance

Name the source and date, do not generalize from visible examples, and abstain when the image coverage or interpretation is uncertain.
