# How can local governments use community feedback?

## Short Answer

Local governments can use community feedback to identify possible barriers, validate data, understand priorities, improve public communication, and inform planning or maintenance review. The government should define how feedback is evaluated and connected to its decisions.

## Significance

Community feedback can reveal conditions or impacts that structured data and imagery do not capture. It also creates an opportunity to explain uncertainty and report back on what was reviewed.

## What This Means

Collect location-specific observations, protect privacy, classify reports consistently, compare them with current data and field evidence, and document whether the result is a correction, an operational action, a planning input, or unresolved information.

## What This Does Not Mean

Feedback does not automatically establish a fact, represent every resident, or require a particular project. Recording feedback does not by itself update OS-CONNECT or satisfy legal engagement requirements.

## How To Use This

Publish the purpose, scope, review process, and limitations. Include affected communities in interpretation, retain the source and date, and communicate outcomes without promising action that has not been approved.

## Example

A city receives reports about a difficult crossing, verifies the location, compares the observation with its dataset, and uses the documented finding to decide whether maintenance, data correction, or further study is appropriate.

## Assistant Guidance

Distinguish reported experience from verified condition and agency decision. Cite the feedback process, avoid claiming consensus from limited input, and abstain when the decision context or review status is unknown.
