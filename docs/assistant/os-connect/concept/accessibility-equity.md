# How does OS-CONNECT support accessibility equity?

## Short Answer

OS-CONNECT can support accessibility-equity work by making pedestrian-network coverage, modeled connections, and selected accessibility information available for comparison and review. It is an input to equity analysis, not a measure of people's lived experience by itself.

## Significance

Shared pedestrian data can make some infrastructure gaps more visible across neighborhoods and help communities, agencies, and advocates identify questions for further investigation.

## What This Means

Users can compare coverage and network conditions across places, combine the data with relevant demographic or community information, and use local knowledge to interpret priorities. Results depend on the dataset, metrics, assumptions, and comparison groups selected.

## What This Does Not Mean

A mapped gap does not prove that a community is underserved, and a high coverage score does not prove equitable access. OS-CONNECT does not determine a distributional outcome, replace community participation, or establish legal compliance.

## How To Use This

Define the equity question before selecting metrics. Document geography, dataset version, profiles, comparison groups, missing data, and limitations; involve affected communities and validate important findings locally.

## Example

A planner compares modeled pedestrian connections near essential destinations in two neighborhoods, then reviews the result with residents and checks priority locations in the field before proposing investments.

## Assistant Guidance

Use careful language such as "the data indicate" or "the model identifies a possible gap." Do not infer resident needs or inequity from one metric, and abstain when the comparison basis or community context is missing.
