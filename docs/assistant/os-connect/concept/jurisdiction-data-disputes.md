# What happens when jurisdictions disagree with the data?

## Short Answer

When a jurisdiction disagrees with OS-CONNECT data, document the specific feature, location, release, evidence, and requested correction. The disagreement should be reviewed through the current data or stewardship process rather than resolved by assumption.

## Significance

Jurisdictions often hold newer or more detailed local information. Recording disagreements helps protect local authority, data lineage, and the accuracy of later releases.

## What This Means

Compare the local source and OS-CONNECT release, check dates and definitions, identify the responsible steward, preserve both records, and document whether the result is a correction, a scoped exception, or an unresolved conflict.

## What This Does Not Mean

Disagreement does not automatically make either source correct or update a public release. It does not establish that a feature is accessible, inaccessible, or legally compliant.

## How To Use This

Provide precise evidence and the local authority for the affected feature. Use the verified reporting route, track the release reviewed, and use the local source separately when a decision requires current jurisdictional information.

## Example

A city identifies a newly built curb ramp missing from the current release. It submits the location, field record, and date, while retaining the local inventory until the release workflow resolves the difference.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not promise that the jurisdiction's view will be accepted or published. Distinguish source disagreement from verification, cite the relevant release guidance, and abstain when scope or authority is unclear.
