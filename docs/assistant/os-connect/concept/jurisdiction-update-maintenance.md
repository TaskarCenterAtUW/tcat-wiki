# How should jurisdictions maintain updates?

## Short Answer

Jurisdictions should maintain updates by assigning owners, recording source and collection dates, reviewing changes, reporting corrections, coordinating boundaries, and tracking which release contains an accepted change.

## Significance

Pedestrian conditions change through construction, maintenance, development, and new observations. A repeatable maintenance practice reduces the gap between local conditions and shared releases.

## What This Means

Define update triggers, review responsibilities, quality checks, issue tracking, version records, and communication or release handoffs. Keep local operational records distinct from shared data until changes are accepted.

## What This Does Not Mean

An update process does not guarantee complete coverage, immediate publication, or synchronization with OpenStreetMap, TDEI, or downstream tools.

## How To Use This

Use the agency's authoritative records for current local operations, submit supported corrections with evidence, and revisit analyses when the relevant release changes.

## Example

A city records a newly rebuilt sidewalk in its local system, validates the geometry, submits the change through the current channel, and notes which public release still lacks it.

## Assistant Guidance

Ask which jurisdiction, source, release, and update responsibility are involved. Do not promise a cadence or acceptance without documentation, and cite the applicable maintenance guidance.
