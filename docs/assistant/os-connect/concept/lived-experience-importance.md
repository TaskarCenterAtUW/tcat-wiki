# Why are lived experiences important?

## Short Answer

Lived experience is important because people who use or avoid pedestrian infrastructure can identify barriers, workarounds, and impacts that structured data, imagery, or automated methods may not capture.

## Significance

Including lived experience helps make accessibility-data questions relevant to actual travel and can reveal where a connected or complete-looking map does not describe practical access.

## What This Means

Collect location-specific observations with accessible and privacy-conscious methods. Preserve the contributor's context, distinguish experience from a dataset value, and use the information to guide review or planning questions.

## What This Does Not Mean

One person's experience does not automatically represent every traveler or prove a legal violation. Lived experience does not replace field, engineering, agency, or data review.

## How To Use This

Ask communities what information and participation formats work for them, avoid unnecessary sensitive details, and communicate how feedback will be reviewed without promising an outcome.

## Example

A person reports that a route shown as connected is difficult to use because of a surface or crossing condition. Reviewers retain the observation and investigate the mapped and physical conditions separately.

## Assistant Guidance

Respect the report without generalizing it. Cite the participation or review process, distinguish reported experience from modeled data, and abstain when context is missing.
