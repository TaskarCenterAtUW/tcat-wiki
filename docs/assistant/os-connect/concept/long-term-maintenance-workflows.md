# What workflows support long-term maintenance?

## Short Answer

Long-term maintenance workflows include monitoring changes, receiving and triaging reports, validating updates, reconciling sources, tracking versions, publishing releases, documenting provenance, and communicating limitations.

## Significance

Recurring workflows keep accessibility data useful after initial collection and make it possible to distinguish new conditions from historical records.

## What This Means

Assign owners and triggers, maintain an issue register, run quality checks, coordinate jurisdictions and communities, record decisions, compare releases, and review whether the workflow still fits its intended use.

## What This Does Not Mean

No workflow guarantees complete or immediate updates, and automation or a single release cannot replace local responsibility and review.

## How To Use This

Start with the data's purpose and risk, define the minimum maintenance cadence and evidence, fund recurring work, and provide clear correction and escalation routes.

## Example

A steward reviews construction reports, compares them with local records, validates priority changes, and records whether each issue appears in a later release.

## Assistant Guidance

Describe the workflow as a documented practice rather than an assumed product feature. Cite current governance information and abstain when owners or cadence are unknown.
