# What does long-term stewardship actually require operationally?

## Short Answer

Long-term stewardship requires people, funding, governance, technical systems, documented schemas, issue and correction workflows, versioned releases, quality review, community participation, and clear communication about limitations.

## Significance

Initial collection does not preserve accessibility data by itself. Stewardship keeps sources interpretable, changes traceable, and known uncertainty visible over time.

## What This Means

Assign owners for source maintenance, validation, support, schema and software changes, release management, licensing, privacy, and public communication. Define triggers, service expectations, escalation, and audit records.

## What This Does Not Mean

Stewardship is not a one-time handoff or a guarantee of complete and current data. It does not require every organization to maintain every feature.

## How To Use This

Match effort to use and risk, fund recurring work, coordinate local and statewide roles, and measure whether corrections and updates are actually incorporated.

## Example

A program maintains an issue register, reviews construction reports, coordinates local owners, validates priority changes, and publishes a release record with known limitations.

## Assistant Guidance

Describe operational requirements without assigning unsupported responsibility. Cite current governance evidence and abstain when ownership, funding, or maintenance arrangements are unknown.
