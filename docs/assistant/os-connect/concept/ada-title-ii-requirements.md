# What does ADA Title II Part B require?

## Short Answer

This is a legal and program-specific question. OS-CONNECT can help organize or screen pedestrian data for planning, but this knowledge base should not summarize an entity's ADA Title II Part B obligations as a complete legal answer. The applicable requirements depend on the public entity, program, facility, facts, and current law.

## Significance

A wrong or overgeneralized legal summary can affect public entities, people with disabilities, and planning decisions. Legal requirements should therefore be checked against current official regulations, guidance, and qualified advice.

## What This Means

For an operational discussion, identify the responsible public entity, program or facility, jurisdiction, dates, and question at issue. Treat inventories, maps, and modeled results as evidence or planning inputs, then consult the current official legal sources and qualified professionals for the determination.

## What This Does Not Mean

OS-CONNECT does not certify compliance, define every Title II obligation, replace a transition plan, or establish what a particular entity must do. A mapped condition is not a legal conclusion.

## How To Use This

Use this article to set the boundary, not to provide a definitive legal interpretation. Record the sources and version used, separate factual data from legal analysis, and escalate jurisdiction-specific questions appropriately.

## Example

An agency may use OS-CONNECT to screen pedestrian features near public facilities before conducting its formal review. The agency still determines the applicable requirements and supporting evidence through its own legal, engineering, and program processes.

## Assistant Guidance

State clearly that legal requirements need current authoritative verification. Do not answer "compliant" or "noncompliant" from OS-CONNECT data, and abstain when the user is asking for legal advice or an entity-specific determination.
