# Is completeness ADA compliance?

## Short Answer

No. Completeness metrics in OS-CONNECT QA/QC reports is a data coverage and modeling concept. ADA compliance is a legal and technical determination made by professional evaluation of real-world conditions.

## Significance

This distinction is important because completeness metrics may be used in accessibility planning. Treating them as compliance findings can mislead users and institutions.

## What This Means

Completeness describes the coverage or presence of mapped data. It can help identify data gaps and guide data-quality-improvement work, but it does not directly relate to any ADA determinations.

## What This Does Not Mean

High completeness does not mean that mapped facilities meet accessibility standards. Low completeness does not establish that a physical feature violates the ADA. Compliance questions require the relevant site facts, standards, and qualified review.

## How To Use This

Use this guidance when a question combines completeness, accessibility, or ADA compliance. State the boundary before describing how the metric may support data-quality or planning work.

## Example

A neighborhood is shown to have high completeness in a QA/QC report. That supports a statement about the amount of data attributes available; it does not establish that sidewalks or other features exist or meet ADA requirements.

## Assistant Guidance

Clearly explain that completeness is a data-coverage concept, not a legal or technical compliance determination. For a site-specific compliance question, recommend review by the responsible jurisdiction and qualified professionals. Do not infer violations from the metric alone - completeness is a property of the dataset, not about the real-world features.
