# Why can a city have high completeness but still accessibility gaps?

## Short Answer

A city can have high completeness while still having accessibility gaps because completeness measures the presence or representation of selected data, not every physical condition, attribute, connection, or lived experience.

## Significance

Completeness is useful for understanding coverage, but separating coverage from accessibility prevents a reassuring score from hiding barriers or missing detail.

## What This Means

Review the metric definition, feature and attribute scope, connectivity, profile assumptions, source date, and local conditions. Pair completeness with field evidence, community reports, and other relevant measures.

## What This Does Not Mean

High completeness is not ADA compliance, proof of accessibility, or evidence that every person can use the network. Low completeness does not prove that infrastructure is absent.

## How To Use This

Use the score to identify data-review priorities, state what it includes and excludes, and validate important conclusions locally.

## Example

A city has mapped most sidewalk geometries but lacks curb-ramp condition data. Its completeness score may be high for geometry while accessibility information remains incomplete.

## Assistant Guidance

Name the completeness measure and scope, avoid broad conclusions from one score, and abstain when the report definition or relevant local evidence is unavailable.
