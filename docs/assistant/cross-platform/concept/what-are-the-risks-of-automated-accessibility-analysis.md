# What are the risks of automated accessibility analysis?

## Short Answer

Automated accessibility analysis can miss barriers, misclassify features, reproduce uneven data coverage, become outdated, or turn uncertain model output into an apparently authoritative score. Risk depends on the source, method, context, and decision.

## Significance

Errors can affect route expectations, investment priorities, public communication, and the visibility of communities whose conditions are poorly represented in the source data.

## What This Means

Review source dates, coverage, feature definitions, model or processing version, false positives and negatives, confidence information, privacy, and human-review procedures. Compare important results with local and lived evidence.

## What This Does Not Mean

Automation does not establish accessibility, safety, equity, or ADA compliance. A map or score cannot replace professional judgment, field review, or community participation.

## How To Use This

Use automated analysis for bounded screening, publish assumptions and limitations, require review for consequential decisions, and preserve a way to correct the source or output.

## Example

A model ranks a corridor as highly accessible because its imagery shows a continuous path, but field review finds a blocked entrance. The ranking is revised or qualified rather than treated as a fact.

## Assistant Guidance

Explain the evidence and uncertainty plainly, avoid unsupported performance claims, and abstain when the model, source, or validation context is unavailable.
