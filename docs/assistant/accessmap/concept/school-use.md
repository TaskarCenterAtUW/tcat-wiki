# How can schools use AccessMap?

## Short Answer

Schools can use AccessMap to compare pedestrian routes to and around a campus under different accessibility preferences. Results can inform questions about access, but should be combined with school, family, community, and transportation planning.

## Significance

Students and families may experience barriers that a general route does not show. Profile-based comparison can help schools identify locations for further review.

## What This Means

- Define the school, origins, destinations, profile, and dataset version.
- Compare routes and identify possible barriers or missing information.
- Verify findings with families, school staff, and local field observations.

## What This Does Not Mean

AccessMap is not a student-safety ranking, eligibility decision, attendance boundary, or substitute for Safe Routes to School planning. A route does not guarantee current conditions.

## How To Use This

Use the output to support discussion and prioritization, while preserving privacy and involving affected communities in decisions.

## Example

A school compares accessible routes from nearby neighborhoods to its entrance, then works with families and the jurisdiction to verify crossings and prioritize improvements.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not promise safety or accessibility outcomes. Cite the route profile and data, ask for the school's context, and recommend local review before operational decisions.
