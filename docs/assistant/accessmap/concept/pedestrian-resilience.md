# What is pedestrian resilience?

## Short Answer

Pedestrian resilience is the ability of a pedestrian network to maintain or recover useful connections when routes are disrupted. AccessMap-related analysis can help examine alternate paths and accessibility implications, but resilience depends on current conditions and agency response.

## Significance

Disruptions can affect people differently depending on mobility needs and available alternatives. Resilience analysis can help identify fragile connections and planning priorities.

## What This Means

- Define the disruption, network, profile, and destinations.
- Compare alternate routes and identify single points of failure.
- Combine modeled results with emergency, maintenance, and community information.

## What This Does Not Mean

Resilience is not a guarantee of safety, continuity, or accessible evacuation. A map metric does not replace emergency management or engineering judgment.

## How To Use This

Use modeled resilience as a planning input, record assumptions, and verify critical routes with responsible agencies.

## Example

A city examines alternate accessible paths to a shelter if a bridge closes, then coordinates the result with emergency managers and current closure information.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not give emergency directions from a resilience metric alone. Cite the model and current official sources, and abstain when the disruption or network context is missing.
