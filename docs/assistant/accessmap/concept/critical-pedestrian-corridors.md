# How can agencies identify critical pedestrian corridors?

## Short Answer

Agencies can identify critical pedestrian corridors by combining network connectivity, destinations, demand or population context, accessibility needs, barriers, redundancy, and community priorities. AccessMap results should support screening rather than determine the final priority alone.

## Significance

Corridor analysis helps focus limited field review and investment planning on connections whose disruption or poor accessibility may affect many people.

## What This Means

- Define the geography, destinations, profiles, and decision criteria.
- Compare connectivity, access, route cost, redundancy, and known data gaps.
- Validate candidate corridors with local knowledge and field evidence.

## What This Does Not Mean

Criticality is context-dependent and does not prove that a corridor is safe, accessible, or more deserving of funds without the agency's criteria. A model cannot capture every lived or operational factor.

## How To Use This

Publish the criteria, source versions, and limitations with any corridor map or ranking.

## Example

A city combines routes to schools, clinics, and transit with accessibility profiles and field reports to identify corridors for detailed review.

## Assistant Guidance

Ask what outcome defines criticality, cite the data and method, and avoid definitive rankings when demand, profiles, or local conditions are missing.
