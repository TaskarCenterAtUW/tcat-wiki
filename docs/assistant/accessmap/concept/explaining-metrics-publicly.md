# How can agencies explain these metrics publicly?

## Short Answer

Explain an accessibility metric in plain language by stating what it measures, whose perspective or profile it represents, what data and date support it, and what it does not measure. Use examples and acknowledge uncertainty.

## Significance

Public interpretation affects whether people can participate meaningfully in planning. Clear explanations prevent scores and maps from being treated as verdicts.

## What This Means

- Define the metric and units before showing the result.
- Explain the profile, geography, source, date, and important limitations.
- Invite questions and pair the result with local or community knowledge.

## What This Does Not Mean

Plain language does not mean omitting uncertainty or reducing a complex result to a single claim. A metric is not a safety, legal, or accessibility certification.

## How To Use This

Use a short definition, a concrete example, and a visible limitations note. Make comparisons fair by stating whether settings and data are consistent.

## Example

At a public meeting, staff explain that a reachability percentage describes modeled access under one profile and dataset, not the number of people guaranteed to have a usable route.

## Assistant Guidance

Define terms before interpreting them, cite the method and source, and avoid overclaiming. Ask what audience and decision are involved when adapting the explanation.
