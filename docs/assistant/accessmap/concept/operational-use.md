# How can cities use AccessMap operationally?

## Short Answer

Cities can use AccessMap operationally to inspect accessibility-aware routes, identify potential network gaps, support planning discussions, and communicate modeled access. Use it as an analytical input alongside local records, field checks, and agency judgment.

## Significance

Operational use connects route analysis to real decisions about services, projects, and communication. Clear limits are important because route outputs depend on data and model assumptions.

## What This Means

- Define the operational question, geography, profile, and dataset version.
- Compare routes or locations under documented scenarios.
- Validate important findings locally and record limitations before acting.

## What This Does Not Mean

AccessMap is not an emergency dispatch system, legal compliance determination, or substitute for field operations. A route does not guarantee that current conditions match the model.

## How To Use This

Use route outputs to screen, compare, and communicate; pair them with responsible agency review and current conditions.

## Example

A city uses profile-based routes to identify a corridor for accessibility review, then verifies crossings and construction conditions before scheduling work.

## Assistant Guidance

Ask what operational decision is involved, cite the route and dataset assumptions, and avoid safety or compliance guarantees. Abstain when the profile, version, or local context is missing.
