# How can AccessMap support emergency planning?

## Short Answer

AccessMap can support emergency planning by helping planners examine modeled pedestrian access and identify routes or gaps for further preparedness work. It should supplement, not replace, emergency-management plans, current alerts, or field decisions.

## Significance

Emergency conditions can change faster than pedestrian datasets and routing models. A cautious planning use can still help agencies identify accessibility questions before an event.

## What This Means

- Define the hazard, area, destinations, profiles, and data date.
- Use modeled routes to identify candidate access needs and vulnerable connections.
- Coordinate with emergency managers and verify current conditions before action.

## What This Does Not Mean

AccessMap is not real-time incident information, dispatch, evacuation authorization, or a safety guarantee. A mapped route may be blocked or unsuitable during an emergency.

## How To Use This

Use it for preparedness and scenario discussion only, with current operational sources and professional emergency guidance.

## Example

A city reviews accessible paths to shelters during preparedness planning, then emergency staff use current incident information and field reports during an event.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not provide emergency instructions from a static route alone. Cite the dataset and emergency sources, and direct urgent users to official emergency channels.
