# What metrics best support accessibility prioritization?

## Short Answer

Accessibility prioritization can use metrics such as reachability, route cost, network gaps, profile differences, redundancy, destinations served, and known data-quality issues. Select metrics based on the agency's purpose and combine them with equity, feasibility, cost, and community criteria.

## Significance

Transparent metrics help agencies explain why locations are being screened or compared. A multi-factor approach reduces the risk that one score hides important accessibility needs.

## What This Means

- Define the decision, population, geography, profiles, and time frame.
- Select and document metrics that match the decision.
- Validate high-priority locations and review results with affected communities.

## What This Does Not Mean

No metric is a substitute for engineering, budgeting, legal review, or community decision-making. A score does not prove that a location is safe, inaccessible, or the highest priority in every context.

## How To Use This

Publish the formula or interpretation, source version, assumptions, and limitations. Revisit priorities when data or conditions change.

## Example

A city combines profile-specific reachability, missing crossing data, community reports, and project feasibility to develop a candidate list for field review.

## Assistant Guidance

Ask what decision the metrics will support, cite the method and source, and abstain from ranking without the criteria and current data.
