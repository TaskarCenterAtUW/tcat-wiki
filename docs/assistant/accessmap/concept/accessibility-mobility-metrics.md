# What metrics matter most for accessible mobility?

## Short Answer

Useful accessible-mobility metrics may include reachable destinations, route cost or length, connectivity, missing or restrictive attributes, profile-specific access, redundancy, and differences between areas or scenarios. The most useful metric depends on the decision and data.

## Significance

Metrics turn complex route results into information that agencies and communities can compare. Choosing them carefully prevents a convenient number from obscuring access barriers or uncertainty.

## What This Means

- Define the population, profile, geography, destinations, and decision.
- Select metrics that reflect the question and document their calculation.
- Pair results with data-quality information and field or community validation.

## What This Does Not Mean

No single metric measures accessibility, safety, equity, or compliance completely. A metric can reflect model and data limitations rather than only physical conditions.

## How To Use This

Explain what the metric measures, what it omits, and how the profile and dataset affect it.

## Example

A planner compares reachable destinations and route costs for two profiles, then checks whether missing data could explain the difference before prioritizing review.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not present a metric as a verdict. Cite the calculation and dataset, ask for the decision context, and abstain from broad conclusions without validation.
