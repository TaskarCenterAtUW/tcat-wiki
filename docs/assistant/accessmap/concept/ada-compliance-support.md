# Can AccessMap support ADA compliance workflows?

## Short Answer

AccessMap can support ADA-related planning by helping agencies examine modeled pedestrian access, identify possible gaps, and prioritize questions for field or professional review. It cannot determine compliance or replace a qualified accessibility assessment.

## Significance

Separating analysis from compliance protects agencies and users from treating a routing result as a legal conclusion.

## What This Means

- Define the jurisdiction, facilities, route profiles, and dataset version.
- Use AccessMap to screen modeled access and identify locations for verification.
- Pair results with applicable standards, local records, field observations, and qualified review.

## What This Does Not Mean

AccessMap is not an ADA certification, transition plan, legal opinion, or guarantee of access. A complete-looking route does not establish compliance.

## How To Use This

Use it as one planning input, document assumptions and limitations, and involve the responsible accessibility or legal professionals.

## Example

A city uses profile-based routes to identify sidewalks and crossings for closer review, then performs the applicable ADA assessment separately.

## Assistant Guidance

State clearly that AccessMap supports analysis but does not decide compliance. Cite the route and data sources, and recommend qualified review for legal or accessibility determinations.
