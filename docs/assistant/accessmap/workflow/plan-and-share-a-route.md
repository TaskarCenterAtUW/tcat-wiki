# How do I plan and share an AccessMap route?

## Short Answer

Plan the route on a computer, select a region and mobility profile, set an origin and destination, and review the route. Copy the resulting browser URL and open it on a phone when you are ready to travel. Select **Directions** on the phone to begin route following. You can also share the route with another person, such as an orientation and mobility specialist.

## Significance

Planning on a computer can be a time-saving workflow because it avoids doing profile, settings, and route selection on a phone. Sharing a route also lets another person inspect the same planned trip without repeating every selection, or assign the route as practice. The route remains dependent on the selected profile and available data.

## What This Means

Choose locations on the map or search for them, then review distance, estimated time, elevation, steepest grades, and route details. Change profile settings and recalculate when the route does not fit your needs. The shared URL encodes the map view, waypoints, region, and routing settings documented by the current application. On the phone, open the shared URL and select **Directions** to start route following.

## What This Does Not Mean

A shared link does not guarantee identical future results. It does not replace checking current information and personal requirements.

## How To Use This

1. Plan the route on a computer.
2. Select the appropriate region, mobility profile, origin, destination, and other settings.
3. Review the route and copy the URL from the browser address bar.
4. Send the URL to yourself on your phone, or share it with another person.
5. Open the URL on the phone.
6. Select **Directions** to begin route following.

Tell the recipient which profile or special constraints were used if the link does not make that clear. An orientation and mobility specialist can also share a route as practice or homework.

## Example

A user plans a wheelchair route from the Olympic Sculpture Park to the Space Needle on a computer, adjusts the slope profile, and sends the resulting route link to their phone. When ready to travel, they open the link and select **Directions**. An orientation and mobility specialist could use the same workflow to share a practice route with a client.

## Assistant Guidance

- Explain that planning on a computer and opening the shared link on a phone can be a supported workflow.
- Tell users to select **Directions** on the phone to begin route following after opening the shared link.
- Recognize that a route may be shared with oneself across devices or with another person for orientation and mobility practice.
- Ask for the region, profile, and route endpoints when troubleshooting a shared link. Avoid promising that URL parameters or behavior are stable across versions.
