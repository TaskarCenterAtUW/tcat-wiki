# What assumptions should planners understand?

## Short Answer

Planners should understand the walkshed's network, profile, cost, destination, barrier, elevation, snapping, coverage, and time assumptions before interpreting an output.

## Significance

These assumptions determine what the result means and whether it fits the planning decision.

## What This Means

Record the dataset and release, origin, profiles, travel limits, network construction, missing-data treatment, destinations, barriers, and comparison method. Validate important results locally.

## What This Does Not Mean

A walkshed is not a direct observation of all travel, a guarantee of accessibility, or a legal, engineering, or equity determination.

## How To Use This

Read the assumptions before using a map, run sensitivity checks, state limitations, and include community and professional interpretation.

## Example

A planner sees a smaller wheelchair walkshed and checks whether the result reflects slope costs, missing crossings, a lower travel limit, or a real condition.

## Assistant Guidance

Name the settings and release, avoid undocumented inferences, and abstain when the assumptions are unavailable.
