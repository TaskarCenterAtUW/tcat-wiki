# What makes a walkshed "good" or "bad"?

## Short Answer

There is no universal "good" or "bad" walkshed. Quality depends on whether the source data, network, profiles, cost rules, destinations, and validation are fit for the intended question.

## Significance

A walkshed may be useful for screening while still being unsuitable for a high-consequence route or compliance decision.

## What This Means

Assess source currency, coverage, geometry, connectivity, attributes, assumptions, reproducibility, validation, and clarity of communication. Compare results with local and lived evidence.

## What This Does Not Mean

A large walkshed is not automatically good and a small one is not automatically bad; each may reflect legitimate network conditions, data gaps, or settings.

## How To Use This

Define acceptance criteria before analysis, document limitations, run sensitivity checks, validate priority locations, and report results with context.

## Example

A planner judges a walkshed useful for identifying candidate crossing reviews but not sufficient to certify a route, because current field conditions were not fully validated.

## Assistant Guidance

Ask what decision the result supports and name the evidence. Avoid a single quality label without criteria and abstain when the data or assumptions are unknown.
