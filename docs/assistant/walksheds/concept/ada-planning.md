# How can walksheds support ADA planning?

## Short Answer

Walksheds can support ADA planning by screening modeled pedestrian reachability, identifying possible network gaps, comparing profiles or scenarios, and helping prioritize locations for review. They are planning inputs, not transition plans or compliance determinations.

## Significance

Network-based analysis can help an agency connect pedestrian conditions to destinations and identify where additional inventory, field work, or investment discussion may be useful.

## What This Means

Define the jurisdiction, facilities, profile, travel limit, dataset version, and decision. Combine walkshed results with barrier inventories, ownership, field evidence, public input, engineering information, and applicable legal guidance.

## What This Does Not Mean

A walkshed does not certify ADA compliance, establish an entity's legal obligations, or prove that an area is inaccessible for every person.

## How To Use This

Use the result to screen and prioritize questions, document assumptions and uncertainty, and obtain qualified legal, engineering, and program review for formal decisions.

## Example

A walkshed identifies limited modeled access to a public facility. The agency validates the route and conditions locally before considering it in a transition-planning process.

## Assistant Guidance

State the profile, cost threshold, network, and release. Do not give legal conclusions from a map, and abstain when the jurisdiction or plan scope is unknown.
