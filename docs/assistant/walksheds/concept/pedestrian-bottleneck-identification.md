# How can walksheds identify pedestrian bottlenecks?

## Short Answer

Walksheds can help identify pedestrian bottlenecks by showing segments, crossings, or connections that constrain modeled reachability or appear on many permitted routes under selected assumptions.

## Significance

Potential bottlenecks can focus review on locations where a missing connection, barrier, crossing, or improvement may affect access to multiple destinations.

## What This Means

Review the network, profile, cost function, origin points, destinations, geography, and source version. Compare bottleneck candidates with redundancy, field conditions, data quality, and community priorities.

## What This Does Not Mean

A narrow crossing is the only modeled connection between two areas. Planners inspect its condition and alternatives before treating it as a candidate bottleneck.

## How To Use This

A modeled bottleneck does not prove congestion, danger, poor design, or importance to every traveler. A low-use segment may still be important to a specific person or destination.

## Example

Use candidates for field review and scenario testing, document thresholds, and avoid converting one metric into an automatic investment ranking.

## Assistant Guidance

Name the metric and network assumptions, cite the release, and abstain from operational or safety claims without current evidence.
