# How can walksheds identify underserved areas?

## Short Answer

Walksheds can help identify areas for underserved-area investigation by showing lower modeled reachability to important destinations, network gaps, or differences across profiles and places. The model should be combined with demographic, service, community, and local evidence.

## Significance

A network-based screen can reveal access differences that simple distance buffers miss and can help prioritize questions for engagement or field review.

## What This Means

Define underserved relative to a documented service, population, geography, profile, destination, travel limit, and comparison. Check coverage, missing data, and community experience before interpreting results.

## What This Does Not Mean

Low modeled reachability does not prove that an area or population is underserved, and a larger walkshed does not prove equitable access or adequate service.

## How To Use This

Use multiple measures, involve affected communities, validate locations locally, and explain whether the result is a data gap, network condition, service issue, or policy question.

## Example

A planner identifies neighborhoods with limited modeled access to clinics and transit, checks the source coverage, and works with residents and agencies before defining a priority area.

## Assistant Guidance

Avoid labeling communities from one metric. Cite data, comparison, and community evidence, and abstain when the definition or context of underserved is not provided.
