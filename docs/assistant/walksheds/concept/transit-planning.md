# How can walksheds support transit planning?

## Short Answer

Walksheds can support transit planning by showing modeled pedestrian access to stops, stations, and other destinations under selected profiles, travel limits, and network assumptions.

## Significance

Transit access depends on both service and the paths used to reach it. A walkshed can reveal pedestrian connections and gaps that stop counts or straight-line buffers miss.

## What This Means

Define stop or station entrances, origins, destinations, profiles, limits, network, transit period, and release. Combine results with current schedules, station pathways, service plans, safety, local data, and community evidence.

## What This Does Not Mean

A transit planner compares wheelchair-profile access to stops along a proposed route and investigates whether differences reflect crossings, barriers, missing data, or service frequency.

## How To Use This

A walkshed does not measure ridership, on-time performance, service quality, or guaranteed transfer accessibility. It does not replace agency transit data or field review.

## Example

Use it as pedestrian-access context, document the transit and network sources separately, validate important transfers, and communicate uncertainty.

## Assistant Guidance

Name the stops, entrances, time period, profile, network, and releases. Avoid conflating reachability with transit service and abstain when feed or pathway context is missing.
