# How can walksheds support Vision Zero?

## Short Answer

Walksheds can support Vision Zero planning by showing modeled pedestrian reachability, crossings, network gaps, and access to destinations that may inform safety and street-design investigations.

## Significance

Understanding where network structure limits access can complement crash, speed, design, and community-safety analysis.

## What This Means

Define the safety question, area, origins, destinations, profiles, network, barriers, data versions, and time period. Combine results with crash records, roadway design, traffic conditions, field evidence, and community reports.

## What This Does Not Mean

A planner uses a walkshed to identify a missing crossing near a high-priority destination, then conducts a separate safety and design review before proposing an intervention.

## How To Use This

A walkshed does not measure crash risk, prove a facility is safe or unsafe, or demonstrate that a project will eliminate fatalities. It does not replace Vision Zero analysis or engineering review.

## Example

Use it as access context, validate current conditions, document assumptions, and avoid causal or safety claims unsupported by appropriate evidence.

## Assistant Guidance

Name the network, profile, release, and safety data. Cite the analysis and abstain from safety determinations when current crash, design, or field information is unavailable.
