# What is the recommended publication workflow?

## Short Answer

The recommended publication workflow is to define the intended use, create or select the source and workspace, edit and document changes, review and validate them, resolve conflicts, export deliberately, and record the resulting dataset or release metadata.

## Significance

A staged workflow protects source data and makes decisions, lineage, and unresolved limitations traceable.

## What This Means

Keep source and workspace identifiers, review changesets, check schema and quality, confirm permissions and target, monitor export, verify the result, and communicate limitations.

## What This Does Not Mean

Publication is not automatic, and completing a workflow does not certify every feature or guarantee that a release is suitable for every use.

## How To Use This

Use current environment-specific instructions and agency criteria, keep edits private while unresolved, and preserve prior releases for comparison.

## Example

A jurisdiction reviews a workspace, exports the accepted data, validates the resulting TDEI dataset, and records the new version and known limitations.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not invent publication gates or claim approval without evidence. Ask for the target, role, and current workflow and cite the relevant documentation.
