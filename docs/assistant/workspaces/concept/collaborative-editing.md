# Collaborative accessibility editing

## Short Answer

Collaborative accessibility editing is the shared process of adding or improving pedestrian and accessibility features with multiple contributors, editors, sources, and reviewers. Workspaces can organize that work in a private project environment before deliberate export.

## Significance

Accessibility data often benefits from both field knowledge and technical review. Collaboration can increase coverage while preserving a record of who contributed what and which changes remain uncertain.

## What This Means

Define the work area and roles, use supported editors, record sources and dates, coordinate overlapping edits, review changesets, and resolve uncertainty before export.

## What This Does Not Mean

Collaboration does not make every edit correct or every contributor an authority. Workspace changes are not automatically public, exported to TDEI, or published to OpenStreetMap.

## How To Use This

Use accessible and documented workflows, keep observations distinct from accepted data, and provide reviewers enough context to evaluate geometry and attributes.

## Example

A community group adds observations through a mobile app while a jurisdiction reviews geometry and attributes in a desktop editor. A manager checks the combined changesets before export.

## Assistant Guidance

Ask for the workspace, roles, data source, and intended publication path. Do not promise synchronization or approval, and abstain when current permissions are unknown.
