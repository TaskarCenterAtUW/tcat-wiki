# When should a city use external GIS tools?

## Short Answer

Use external GIS tools when the work requires agency-specific analysis, authoritative operational records, advanced geoprocessing, large-data management, cartography, or workflows not supported by Workspaces. Use Workspaces when collaborative feature editing and review are the primary need.

## Significance

Choosing the right environment reduces duplication and keeps editing, analysis, stewardship, and publication responsibilities clear.

## What This Means

Compare the task, data format, scale, permissions, collaboration needs, validation, lineage, analysis tools, and export requirements. Confirm compatibility before moving data between systems.

## What This Does Not Mean

External GIS is not automatically more authoritative, and Workspaces is not a replacement for every agency GIS function. Either tool still requires source, version, quality, and access review.

## How To Use This

Use a documented division of labor: collaborative edits and review in Workspaces when appropriate, agency records and specialized analysis in GIS, and deliberate validated export between them.

## Example

A city uses Workspaces for shared sidewalk edits and review but uses its GIS for asset management, spatial joins, capital analysis, and official records.

## Assistant Guidance

Ask for the decision, data, users, scale, and output. Avoid declaring one tool universally better and abstain when workflow requirements are unclear.
