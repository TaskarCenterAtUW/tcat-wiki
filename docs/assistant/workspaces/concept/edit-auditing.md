# How can edits be audited?

## Short Answer

Edits can be audited by reviewing changesets, feature history, authorship, timestamps, source metadata, comments, geometry and attribute differences, review decisions, and export records available in Workspaces.

## Significance

An audit trail helps managers verify process compliance, investigate discrepancies, and determine which changes require clarification before release.

## What This Means

Define the audit scope, identify the workspace and source release, inspect the relevant records, compare before and after states, document findings, and preserve unresolved questions.

## What This Does Not Mean

An audit does not prove that the physical condition is correct or that every edit was observed. Metadata and review records are evidence, not legal certification.

## How To Use This

Use the current interface and project acceptance criteria, sample high-risk changes, protect personal information, and record who performed the audit and when.

## Example

A manager audits a changeset by checking its contributors, sources, affected curb ramps, comments, and review outcome before authorizing export.

## Assistant Guidance

Do not claim an audit was complete without defining its scope. Cite the records and criteria, and abstain when history or workspace access is unavailable.
